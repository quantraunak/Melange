# Why viral consumer social apps of 2020–2026 faded or died

Researched 2026-10-20. Numbers come mostly from third-party estimators (Appfigures, Apptopia, Sensor Tower, Similarweb) or from company claims, and the two often disagree. Where a figure is weak or single-source, it is marked as such.

---

## Part 1: Case files

### Clubhouse (live audio rooms, 2020–21)
- **Peak:** 8.7M downloads in June 2021, its biggest month (Appfigures). Around 10M weekly actives at peak (SF Standard). $4B valuation at the April 2021 Series C.
- **Later:** 332K downloads in June 2022, down about 95% (Appfigures, https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20220701). Cut more than 50% of staff in April 2023 (https://sfstandard.com/2023/04/27/whos-listening-once-buzzy-clubhouse-lays-off-half-its-employees). Pivoted to async voice "Chats" among small friend groups. No reliable 2025–26 MAU figure exists.
- **Loop:** Drop into a live room and hear famous people. FOMO from ephemerality and invite scarcity.
- **Why it faded:** (1) The occasion was COVID lockdown boredom; the founders themselves said "usage has declined as COVID-related restrictions have been lifted." (2) Live-only synchronous content needs everyone online at once and leaves nothing to browse later. (3) Twitter Spaces, Facebook Live Audio Rooms, Spotify Greenroom and LinkedIn copied it within months. (4) Discovery got worse as it scaled: there were too many low-quality rooms and the celebrities left.
- **Acquisition:** None.

### BeReal (once-a-day dual-camera photo, 2022)
- **Peak:** About 15M DAU in October 2022 (Apptopia via NYT). Some estimates say 20M. A 73.5M MAU peak figure is single-source and should not be relied on.
- **Later:** Under 6M DAU by March 2023, a drop of about 61% (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal; NYT/Apptopia). After Voodoo bought it, the company claimed 40M "active users," which TechCrunch could not verify (https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/10/bereal-which-says-it-has-40m-monthly-users-is-rolling-out-ads-in-the-u-s). A Sensor Tower figure of about 12M DAU in May 2026 is secondary and weakly sourced.
- **Loop:** A daily push at a random time, and you can't see friends' posts until you post your own. Post-to-view is a strong reciprocity gate.
- **Why it faded:** (1) It was a single feature, and the daily post became a chore that repeated with no variety. (2) For a long time it had no revenue, so cash ran out. Press reported it was seeking a buyer as funding dried up. (3) Incumbents copied it within months: TikTok Now (September 2022, killed June 2023), Instagram Candid, and Snapchat dual camera. Notably, the clones failed too, which says the mechanic itself had a short half-life. (4) There was no second reason to open the app beyond the daily ping.
- **Acquisition:** Voodoo bought it in June 2024 for €500M, reportedly with only about a third paid in cash up front (https://voodoo.io/news/voodoo-acquires-bereal). Why: Voodoo is a hyper-casual games company diversifying into consumer apps and wanted a monetizable Gen-Z audience. It added ads (US launch April 2025, 200+ campaigns) and reported about €30M revenue in 2025 against zero at acquisition. That revenue figure is secondary.

### Dispo (delayed-develop "disposable camera," 2021)
- **Peak:** Number one on the App Store around its February 2021 beta. Raised $20M at a $200M valuation (Spark Capital).
- **Later:** In March 2021, co-founder David Dobrik's Vlog Squad scandal broke. Spark pulled its funding, Dobrik left, and the app was review-bombed (https://petapixel.com/2021/03/22/dispo-loses-top-investor-and-dobrik-leaves-company-amid-controversy). It faded to niche status. No reliable later download numbers were found.
- **Loop:** Shoot photos and wait until 9am the next day for them to "develop." Shared rolls with friends.
- **Why it faded:** (1) It depended on a single founder-celebrity for distribution and brand. (2) The aesthetic gimmick was easy to copy, since filter apps already existed. (3) It had no recurring social occasion.
- **Acquisition:** None found.

### Poparazzi (only friends can post photos of you, 2021)
- **Peak:** Number one on the App Store in May 2021. About 4M MAU at peak. 5M+ (company) or 6.2M (Apptopia) lifetime installs. $15M Series A from Benchmark.
- **Later:** About 2,000–3,000 MAU. Shut down in 2023, with content export available until June 30, 2023, and capital returned to investors (https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/01/once-hot-photo-sharing-social-app-poparazzi-is-shutting-down/).
- **Loop:** Friends tag and post photos of you, which inverts the selfie.
- **Why it faded:** (1) It had no recurring occasion. You need to be physically with friends taking candid shots. (2) Density mattered: it only works if many of your actual friends are on it. (3) Its novelty was an inversion of an existing behavior, not a new need. MAU fell by about 99.9%.
- **Acquisition:** None. It wound down.

### Gas (anonymous compliment polls for teens, 2022)
- **Peak:** 1M DAU within about two months of its August 2022 launch. 3.1M downloads in November 2022 (data.ai). Revenue from "God Mode" at $6.99 a week: the company claimed $3M in under three months and $5M cumulative by December 2022, while Appfigures estimated about $100K net, a large gap.
- **Later:** About 2,900 downloads the next month (TechCrunch, citing data.ai). Discord acquired it in January 2023 and shut it down on November 7, 2023 "due to a steep decline in users" (https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago).
- **Loop:** Answer flattering polls about classmates seeded from your school's contacts. You get notified that "someone said X about you," and paying reveals hints.
- **Why it faded:** (1) It was a deliberately designed school-semester spike. The founder, Nikita Bier, ran the same playbook with tbh (sold to Facebook in 2017 and also shut down). (2) A viral false rumor tied it to human trafficking (October 2022), which spooked parents. (3) The polls were repetitive, so the novelty ran out in weeks.
- **Acquisition:** By Discord, explicitly for the engineering team. Citron's memo said they would run Gas only "until growth slowed." This was an acqui-hire exit, not a product bet.

### NGL (anonymous questions via Instagram Story links, 2022)
- **Peak:** Top of the charts in 2022, riding Instagram Stories.
- **Later:** In July 2024, a $5M settlement with the FTC and the LA DA, plus a ban on users under 18 (https://abc7news.com/post/popular-app-banned-hosting-young-teens-groundbreaking-settlement/15048436/). The FTC alleged NGL sent fake "anonymous" messages ("I know what you did") to drive engagement, then charged users to "unmask" senders but returned only vague hints.
- **Loop:** Post a link and receive anonymous messages. Pay to see who sent them.
- **Why it faded:** (1) Organic anonymous messages were too sparse, so the company faked them. (2) Monetization was built on a dishonest reveal. (3) Its core market was minors, which drew regulators. This is the canonical case of "synthetic engagement leads to legal death."
- **Acquisition:** None.

### Yik Yak relaunch (anonymous location feed, 2021–23)
- **History:** The original shut down in 2017 after bullying and threats drove schools to ban it. Block bought the IP for about $1M. A new team bought the brand in February 2021 and relaunched in August 2021.
- **Later:** Rival Sidechat acquired it in March 2023 in a deal closer to an acqui-hire. Users were moved to a Sidechat-clone UI on the same servers and protested (https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/16/anonymous-app-sidechat-picks-up-rival-yik-yak-and-users-arent-happy/).
- **Why it faded:** (1) Anonymous campus feeds are bounded by campus density, and two apps split the same campuses. (2) The moderation burden is heavy. (3) Nostalgia drove re-downloads but not retention.
- **Acquisition:** By a direct competitor, to consolidate density.

### IRL (group events and chat, 2021–23)
- **Peak (claimed):** 20M users. Over $1B valuation (SoftBank Vision Fund 2, June 2021). Raised about $200M.
- **Reality:** A board investigation found about 95% of the 20M were bots or automated accounts. The real figure may have been around 1M. It shut down in June 2023. The CEO was suspended for misconduct, and the SEC had opened an inquiry in 2022 (https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/26/irl-shut-down-fake-users/).
- **Why it died:** Growth was manufactured to hit fundraising metrics. Once the vanity metrics were exposed, there was nothing underneath.
- **Acquisition:** None.

### Lemon8 (ByteDance lifestyle/Pinterest-ish, 2023–25)
- **Peak:** Heavily paid-pushed in the US in 2023. About 12.5M global MAU in December 2024 (Sensor Tower), and about 1M US DAU (Similarweb) during the TikTok-ban scare.
- **Later:** Its 2023 US slump halved DAU in a few months (Similarweb via Rest of World, https://restofworld.org/2023/lemon8-app-bytedance-user-decline/). Post-2025 data was not found.
- **Why it struggled:** (1) Users were bought with paid creator seeding, not pulled in organically. (2) It had no differentiated loop against Instagram and Pinterest. (3) Its fate depends on its parent company's politics.
- **Acquisition:** Not applicable (ByteDance-owned).

### Hive Social (Twitter alternative, November–December 2022)
- **Peak:** About 1M to 2M users in weeks during the Musk-Twitter exodus. It was run by about two people.
- **Later:** Security researchers (Zerforschung) found that all data, including DMs, private posts and deleted messages, was accessible. It shut its servers for about two weeks in December 2022 and came back with DMs disabled (https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/twitter-alternative-hive-shuts-down-its-app-to-fix-critical-security-issues/). The momentum went to Mastodon and later Bluesky and Threads.
- **Why it faded:** Exogenous refugee growth hit an infrastructure that wasn't ready, and the result was a trust collapse at the moment of peak attention.

### Vero (ad-free chronological Instagram alternative, February–March 2018, pre-window but instructive)
- **Peak:** Went from about 0.5M to 3M registrations in weeks and reached number one on the App Store.
- **Later:** Servers buckled, the "free for the first million" subscription promise confused users, and a founder controversy followed. It faded within about a month (https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=109053).
- **Why:** It was an "alternative to X" defined by a grievance, not a new behavior. The grievance didn't move whole friend graphs.

### Tinder (dating, decline 2023–26)
- **Numbers:** Tinder payers fell from about 9.2M (Q3 2025, down 7% year over year) to 8.8M (Q4 2025, down 8%). Match total payers were 13.8M in Q4 2025, down 5%. Match cut 13% of staff in May 2025 (https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/match-group-to-cut-13-of-workforce-as-paid-users-decline/). Management guided that 2026 Tinder revenue declines would be similar to 2025.
- **Why:** (1) Swipe fatigue, and the gamified loop burned out its users. (2) Aggressive paywalling of features like seeing who liked you, Gold, and the $500/month Platinum tier made the free product worse, which degrades the pool. (3) Gen Z shifted to meeting through friends, run clubs and IRL events. (4) Safety and bot fatigue.

### Bumble (dating, decline 2023–26)
- **Numbers:** Total users went from 58M (2023) to 50M (2024). Payers were flat at about 4M in Q1 2025, while revenue per payer fell 7.3%. It cut about 30% of staff (about 240 people) in June 2025 (https://fortune.com/2025/06/25/bumble-layoffs-surprise-jpmorgan-analysts). It then shrank its paying base on purpose, for "quality over quantity."
- **Why:** The same swipe fatigue, plus removing its own differentiator (relaxing the women-message-first rule in 2024), plus monetization of the funnel.

### Venmo social feed (social-payments experiment)
- **What happened:** In July 2021 it removed the global public feed of strangers' transactions after reporters found President Biden's account through public friend lists (https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/07/venmo-takes-another-step-toward-privacy). The social layer shrank to friends-only.
- **Lesson:** Social content that is a by-product of a sensitive action, such as money, location or dating, carries creepiness risk that grows with reach. Cash App never leaned into a public feed for the same reason.

### Lapse (delayed "disposable" photos plus mandatory invites, 2023–24)
- **Peak:** About 218K downloads in one day in October 2023 (Appfigures).
- **Later:** About 63K a day by mid-November 2023, down 70% (https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20231117/5-lapse-loses-momentum-as-downloads-drop-70). It still raised a $30M Series A in February 2024 (Greylock/DST, about $150M valuation). No 2025–26 data was found, so it is fading rather than confirmed dead.
- **Loop:** You had to invite 5 friends to unlock posting, which forced contact-book spam.
- **Why it faded:** Forced-invite growth produced resentful, low-intent users. Appfigures called it "a dark pattern that inevitably turned off users." It also shared Dispo's problem of being a gimmick with no recurring occasion.

### 2024–2026 one-hit viral apps
- **Sora app (OpenAI, launched September 30, 2025):** Monthly downloads went 2.5M (October 2025), 3.2M (November), 2.2M (December), 1.2M (January 2026, down 45% month over month). It fell out of the US Top 100. Only about $1.4–2.1M lifetime in-app revenue. OpenAI announced the shutdown on March 24, 2026, reportedly while compute cost about $1M a day (https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/29/openais-sora-app-is-struggling-after-its-stellar-launch/; https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html). Why: AI-generated "slop" feed with no friend graph and no reason to return. Content cost scales with usage while revenue does not. Google and Meta matched the model.
- **Tea (anonymous women-only "are we dating the same guy," July 2025):** Downloads rose 525% week over week and it hit number one in the US. Then a misconfigured storage bucket leaked about 72K images, including about 13K ID selfies, and later DMs. That brought about 10 class actions (https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/26/dating-safety-app-tea-breached-exposing-72000-user-images). It briefly stayed at number two, because notoriety drove installs, but the brand is now toxic. A male clone, TeaOnHer, was also breached. Why: an app selling safety collected IDs and ran with no security engineering, plus defamation risk.
- **noplace (MySpace-ish, July 2024):** Number one on the App Store within days, with a 500K waitlist claim. No published retention data, and it dropped out of the top charts quickly. The fade is qualitative only.
- **Airchat (voice-to-text feed, April 2024):** About 45–50K downloads at its buzz peak (Sensor Tower). It was confined to a tech-VC bubble, and the "Clubhouse again" framing was immediate. No confirmed shutdown was found.

## Part 2: Recurring failure modes and the design choice that prevents each

### FM1. A novelty mechanic with no recurring occasion
Examples: Poparazzi (4M to about 2K MAU), Dispo, Lapse (down 70% in 6 weeks), Gas (3.1M to 2.9K monthly downloads), Sora (down 45% month over month).
The mechanic is fun the first ten times, but nothing in users' lives pulls them back.
**Prevention:** Anchor the app to an occasion that already recurs outside it, such as a weekly event, a shoot, a game, a class or a trip. Make the app the tool for that occasion, not a toy. Measure D30 retention per cohort before measuring downloads.

### FM2. A single feature that incumbents can clone
Examples: Clubhouse (Spaces, Live Audio Rooms within months), BeReal (TikTok Now, IG Candid, Snap dual camera), Stories-era clones, Sora (Google, Meta).
**Prevention:** The moat must be accumulated state that a clone can't import: a history of completed real-world transactions, reputation and reviews, saved assets, and a graph of who has worked with whom. A feature is copyable. A ledger of completed things is not.

### FM3. A context-bound spike mistaken for product-market fit
Examples: Clubhouse (COVID lockdown), Hive (Twitter exodus), Vero (Instagram algorithm grievance), Lemon8 (TikTok-ban scare), Tea (one viral news moment).
**Prevention:** Segment cohorts by acquisition wave and only count retention from users who arrived without the external shock. Don't raise money or hire against shock-driven peaks.

### FM4. Density failure, where the graph doesn't transfer
Examples: Poparazzi (needs your real friends there), Yik Yak and Sidechat (bounded by campus, two apps splitting the same campuses), Airchat (stuck in a VC bubble), Hive and Vero (individuals moved, friend graphs didn't).
**Prevention:** Launch in one tight, bounded community, such as one school, scene or city, and reach usable density there before expanding. Design single-player value so the app is useful with zero friends on it, as a tool first and a network second.

### FM5. Safety, privacy and anonymity blowups
Examples: NGL (FTC, $5M, under-18 ban), Tea (72K images leaked, about 10 class actions), Hive (all DMs exposed), Gas (trafficking rumor), Yik Yak 1.0 (bullying bans), Venmo (Biden's account found through public friend lists), Dispo (founder scandal).
**Prevention:** Use real identity, not anonymity. Make content private or friends-only by default. Don't collect IDs you can't secure. Have a security review before any growth push. Keep minors out unless you are prepared for COPPA-grade compliance. Avoid tying the brand to one creator.

### FM6. Synthetic or forced engagement
Examples: NGL (fake messages), IRL (95% bots), Lapse (forced 5-invite gate), Gas (rumor-amplified paid "God Mode" hints).
**Prevention:** Count only engagement between two real humans. Don't gate core value on invites. Audit your own metrics, since investors eventually will.

### FM7. Monetization pressure that corrupts the core loop, or no monetization at all
Examples: Tinder (payers down 8% year over year while paywalls rose), Bumble (revenue per payer down 7.3%, 30% layoffs), NGL (pay-to-unmask), BeReal (zero revenue, then a forced sale; about a third of the €500M paid in cash), Sora (about $1M a day in compute against about $2M lifetime revenue).
**Prevention:** Charge the party that gets business value, such as the pro or creator who earns from the outcome, and keep the social or matching side free. Make the unit cost per active user bounded, and avoid per-view generative costs unless they are paid for.

### FM8. Acquisition as a soft death
Examples: Gas (Discord acqui-hire, killed in 9 months), Yik Yak (absorbed by Sidechat), tbh (Facebook, killed), BeReal (sold to a games publisher for ad monetization).
Acquirers bought teams or audiences, not the loop.
**Prevention:** This mode is downstream of FM1, FM2 and FM7. An app with recurring occasions and its own revenue doesn't need a rescue buyer.

## Caveats
- Peak and current figures come from different estimators (Appfigures, Apptopia, Sensor Tower, Similarweb) and from company claims, so they are not strictly comparable.
- BeReal's 73.5M MAU peak and its 2026 DAU are weak. Gas revenue claims ($5M from the company vs. about $100K net from Appfigures) differ by 50x. The noplace and Airchat fades have no hard numbers.
- The "Cash App social" item found no discrete Cash App experiment, so Venmo's public-feed removal is used as the social-payments case.
