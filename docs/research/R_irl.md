# What works in IRL / friendship / events businesses for ages 18–30 (as of 2026-10-20)

Method: web search and fetch on 2026-10-20. Each number has its source and date. Where sources conflict, both are shown. Figures that are "self-reported" or come from an "aggregator" are labeled that way. **Gap:** Few companies publish retention or repeat-attendance figures. Where a cell says "not found", I searched and found nothing.

## 1. Company-by-company

### Timeleft (dinner with 5 strangers, Wednesdays 7pm, Paris HQ)
- **Revenue:** €18M ARR in Aug 2025, about 20 months after the dinner pivot. Earlier milestones: €20k MRR in Dec 2023, €1M monthly revenue about 16 months after launch, and €125 in the first test week (May 2023). Source: [Tim Frin Substack, 2025-10-09](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting). arr.club and Signalbase repeat the €18M ARR / 150k monthly users figures. Neither source is primary.
- **Scale:** 150k monthly participants (Aug 2025). City count peaked at 320–325 in 2024, then was cut back to about 200–220. The company's internal rule for a viable city is 50+ weekly participants. About 100 staff. 15–20k reviews a week (same Substack). Other outlets report 278 cities in 61 countries ([Connexion France](https://connexionfrance.com/news/new-trend-serves-companionship-up-on-a-plate-in-france/682998)) and "300+ cities, 11,500 strangers every Wednesday" ([Alta](https://www.altaonline.com/dispatches/a63924701/strangers-in-the-night/)).
- **Pricing:** Now sold by subscription only, in 1/3/6/12-month plans. Price varies by city, and food is paid separately ([help center](https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/17677297227164-Subscription-Only-Experience)). Reported prices: about $16 single ticket or $26/month unlimited (Alta); €19.99/month for 4–5 dinners ([Le Bonbon](https://en.lebonbon.fr/paris/society/a-crazy-concept-of-dinners-between-strangers-for-singles-is-coming-to-paris)); €12.99 single ([Brussels Times](https://brusselstimes.com/1160308/table-for-six-strangers-fighting-urban-loneliness-over-dinner)). It also earns from restaurant partnerships.
- **Repeat use:** Average use rose from 1.4 to 2.2 experiences per month per user (Substack). The help center says users who attend 2 or more dinners a month are "happiest". No churn figure is published.
- **Acquisition:** Paid ads only. There are no city managers or ambassadors. Each new city is launched with a paid test, and the app is integrated only if CAC works (Substack). It launched about one new city per day through 2024.
- **Funding:** $7M Series A in Jul 2024 (CB Insights). Dealroom says no external funding.
- **Failures and lessons:** Opening 325 cities stretched the operation thin, and small cities "fizzled". In small cities, users got bored of seeing the same ~10 people every week. The low-code stack broke, and the migration took 8 months. Attendees' criticism: the personality mix "felt like roulette" ([Gazetteer SF, 2025-10-23](https://sf.gazetteer.co/pay-to-play)).

### 222 (AI-curated groups of strangers sent to public venues; NYC-centric)
- **Revenue:** "Several million dollars annually, close to cash-flow positive". 17 markets, "thousands of participants weekly" ([Forbes Italia, 2026-07-13](https://forbes.it/2026/07/13/niente-swipe-cene-di-gruppo-e-ia-cosi-queste-startup-stanno-reinventando-le-app-di-incontri/)). Gazetteer (Oct 2025) reported "hundreds of thousands" of users in 9 markets and a 17-person team.
- **Price:** Early on, $2.22 per event (ages 18–27 only). In Oct 2025, a $17 curation fee or a $22/month subscription, with food (about $40) paid separately (Gazetteer). In 2026, $22.22 per event or $22.22/month ([TechCrunch, 2026-04-05](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/05/as-people-look-for-ways-to-make-new-friends-here-are-the-apps-promising-to-help/)). It also charges venues, restaurants and organizers.
- **Funding:** $10.1M Series A in Dec 2025, led by Upfront. $16.8M raised in total.
- **Retention mechanism:** Founders say the matching algorithm needs feedback from 5 or more events, so retention is built into the product. Plus-ones are allowed. Users go through vetting. The venue is revealed on the day.
- **Weakness:** A reporter's view: "pleasant and polite… didn't get the impression many formed deep friendships" (Gazetteer).

### Pie (Andy Dunn, Chicago; events, now moving to "communities")
- **Users:** 130k MAU in Mar 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/andy-dunns-new-app-pie-uses-ai-to-help-you-make-friends)). About 300k users in Aug 2026 ([TechCrunch, 2026-08-25](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3156057)). Strongest in Chicago, NYC and LA.
- **Funding:** about $24M (Forerunner-led $11.5M Series A in Sep 2024, plus Lightspeed and Accel).
- **Supply model:** Pie runs no events of its own. A $1M Creator Fund pays hosts $5–10 per RSVP ([Fitt Insider, 2024-09-24](https://insider.fitt.co/pie-raises-11-5m-for-irl-friendships/)). "Sparked by Pie" sorts attendees into groups of 6 using a quiz and opens a group chat before the event.
- **What moved engagement:** In Dec 2025, Pie started showing which of a user's contacts are attending an event. Time in app rose from 2 to 10 minutes (TechCrunch Aug 2026). It hired the Zoomies run-club founder as co-founder and CMO. In Aug 2026 it pivoted to "Community Homes", which makes events a byproduct of groups.
- **Failure mode:** Business Insider called it "mostly a directory". Attendees rarely mentioned Pie, and few events appeared early on ([BI via AOL, 2024-11-11](https://www.aol.com/news/tried-pie-social-million-dollar-130801270.html)).

### Bumble BFF (relaunch Sep 2025, built on Geneva)
- Relaunched on 2025-09-18, built on Geneva (acquired in 2024, reportedly for about $17M). Geneva then shut down ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)). Group discovery did not arrive until Feb 2026.
- **Revenue:** Geneva had made **$0 revenue** as of 2025-06-30. Bumble's Q1/Q2 2026 8-Ks say it "has not sought to generate revenue from the BFF app" and leave BFF out of its KPIs ([Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001830043/000183004326000099/bmbl-ex991prxq22026.htm)). The parent company is shrinking: paying users fell to 3.17M from 4.01M a year earlier (Q1 2026).
- **Lesson:** A large incumbent with a well-known brand still could not make a free swipe-for-friends app pay.

### Thursday (London; dating app turned IRL events)
- 2M users and a £2.5M seed. The app shut in Jan 2025 over "rapidly declining consumer interest", after layoffs and monthly losses. The company moved to ticketed weekly Thursday Events, trips and merchandise ([GDI, 2025-01-10](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/thursday-shutters-dating-app-to-shift-focus-on-real-world-events/)).
- In Feb 2026 it claimed a **$50M valuation**, bought thursday.com and started a Series A ([GDI, 2026-02-23](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/thursday-acquires-thursday-com-domain-hits-50m-in-value/)). No revenue or attendance figures are published.
- **Lesson:** The brand and the weekly ritual survived. The swipe app did not.

### Hinge / Match Group IRL efforts
- **Hinge "One More Hour":** a $1M fund announced Dec 2023, with grants of up to $25k each to groups running recurring, affordable meetups in NYC, LA and ATL. It expanded to the UK in 2025 and France in 2026. The 2026 fund is $1.5M, and $2.3M has been given out in total ([GDI](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/hinge-expands-one-more-hour-initiative-to-france/)). This is brand spend, not a revenue line. Hinge itself grew revenue 28% in Q1 2026 (Forbes Italia).
- **Tinder Events tab:** piloted in LA in Mar 2026. **71% of active LA users aged 18–24 engaged with the tab. 60+ events drew engagement from 66% of eligible active users.** It is expanding to 26 cities (TechCrunch) or 9 (Marketing Dive). The events are run by third-party organizers ([WERSM](https://wersm.com/tinder-expanding-irl-events-gen-z-dating-apps/)).
- Eventbrite reports that events using "meet-cute" language grew 117% in 2026.

### Posh (Gen Z nightlife and event ticketing with a discovery feed)
- 2024 revenue was about $10M on $83M+ in ticket sales. Cumulative: $350M GMV, 25M tickets, about $40M revenue. About 50k organizers; top organizers have done over $10M each. Users: 6–8M. **Take rate: about 10% plus $0.99 per ticket.** $37M Series B led by FirstMark in Mar 2026 ([Fortune, 2026-03-19](https://www.fortune.com/2026/03/19/exclusive-posh-lands-37m-series-b-what-are-we-doing-tonight-problem)). Built In's figure of "$65.2M revenue in 2025" is unverified.
- **Model:** Began as "Shopify for events" for organizers and now emphasizes demand through a discovery feed. CEO: "you have to own the transaction first."

### Luma
- Only $3M in disclosed funding (seed, 2020). One report (Jan 2026) says it runs with about 6 full-time staff. An unverified figure: about 2M event sign-ups a month and 5x user growth from 2023 to 2024 ([party.pro](https://old.party.pro/luma/)). It is the default platform for tech and AI events. It charges hosts a subscription plus a fee on paid tickets. **No verified revenue figure.** The lesson is the lean economics: one vertical (tech) and almost no staff.

### Partiful (invites; events side)
- $20M a16z round in 2022 at a $100M valuation. **Q1 2025: about 500k MAU, up 400% year over year** (Sensor Tower via [CNBC/NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/)). Added 2M users in 2025. Comparison: Eventbrite about 4.4M MAU (+2%), Evite over 20M MAU.
- **Monetization:** It launched paid ticketing on 2026-06-02, its first major revenue product after 6 years free. It does not publish a take rate. Luma says it has seen "about 10% + $2" ([Luma](https://help.luma.com/p/luma-vs-partiful)).
- **Growth engine:** every invite sent brings in new users. It has never needed matching with strangers.

### Eventbrite (the incumbent)
- **FY2025 net revenue $291.8M, down 10%,** with fewer paid tickets. Year-to-date paid tickets through Q3 2025 fell 6% to 58.3M ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1475115/000147511526000005/eb-20251231.htm)). It agreed to be sold to **Bending Spoons for about $500M** on 2025-12-02, against a $1.76B IPO valuation in 2018 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251202408560/en/)).
- **Lesson:** A general-purpose ticketing tool with no community or discovery loop loses ground to Posh, Partiful and Luma in the young segment.

### Meetup (decline)
- Bending Spoons acquired it, moved operations to Europe, and cut most of the US team ([Silicon Republic](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/?p=1066743)). Organizers report steep Meetup Pro price increases, and some say they are leaving (single blog source, [andypiper.co.uk](https://andypiper.co.uk/?p=5834)). On 2026-01-15, Meetup deactivated accounts inactive for 3 years or more, so its member counts are inflated by dead accounts ([Meetup product updates](https://product.meetup.com/date/2025/12)). No official totals are published.
- **Lesson:** Charging the volunteer hosts who supply the events kills supply.

### Run clubs (Strava Year in Sport)
- **2024 report:** run-club participation +59% and group runs of 10+ people +18%. **1 in 5 respondents had dated someone met at a run club.** 66% of Gen Z made friends through fitness groups, and 55% say socializing is their main reason ([Athletech](https://athletechnews.com/strava-2024-fitness-report-highlights-trends)).
- **2025 report:** new running clubs 3.5x, hiking clubs 5.8x. Gen Z is 39% more likely than Gen X to use fitness to meet people, and 64% would rather spend on gear than on dates. Strava has 180M users (Dec 2025) and a $2.2B valuation ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/13/strava-ipo-ceo-michael-martin-gen-z-running-clubs-marathons-dating-apps)). NYC Marathon lottery applicants topped 200k, up 22%.
- **Money:** the clubs themselves are mostly free. Revenue goes to brands and retailers through sponsorship (Fleet Feet, Asics, On, Hoka). No public financials for the clubs. The activity gives people a reason to show up, and the clubs bring brands an audience.

### Supper clubs / dinners with strangers (non-VC)
- Nine26 (NYC): waitlist of about 10k ([Cornell Sun, 2026-03](https://www.cornellsun.com/article/2026/03/from-hotelie-to-hosting-new-york-s-hottest-dinner-parties-meet-eddy-cruz-17)). Bazaar Supper Club (Brooklyn): 18 seats in an apartment, waitlist over 10k ([American Bazaar, 2026-05-08](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/05/08/who-is-nirupa-konejetti-turns-nyc-apartment-into-supper-club-480398/)). ATX Supper Club grew from 1 host to 6 and has 7.5k IG followers. The Free Press runs supper clubs as a media extension.
- The precedent failure is Dinner Lab: a membership supper club ($100–175/yr) that raised $2.1M in 2014 and later closed. Scarcity creates demand but does not scale beyond the host.

### Social clubs
- **Soho House:** 270k+ members (Jun 2025). Taken private by an MCR-led group at $9/share, about $2.7B EV (Aug 2025). The under-27 tier lasts until age 30, and its food-and-drink discount was cut from 50% to 25% for members approved from 2025-01-24 ([Soho House](https://sohohouse.com/discounts)). So the youth funnel still exists, but its benefits are being reduced.
- **New-wave clubs:** Zero Bond reportedly charges about $4.4k/yr in dues with a 10k+ waitlist. Club membership among 18–29s has reportedly doubled since 2016 (industry marketing source, low confidence). Wellness clubs run up to $750/month.
- **Clockout** (Gen Z offline networking clubs): **$1.1M ARR within 10 months of monetizing, at $99/yr freemium. 600k+ downloads. ~400k invites sent by users in 2025.** $3.5M round ([Dealroom, 2026-10-02](https://dealroom.co/news/158832-clockout-raises-3-5m-to-help-gen-z-build-careers-offline/)).
- I could not identify a specific startup named "Neighborhood" from the brief, so it is not covered.

### "IRL" the app (cautionary case)
- Raised $170M and was valued as a unicorn. It claimed 12M "organic" users but spent about $6M on incentivized downloads. A board investigation found **95% of users were bots or automated**. It shut down in Jun 2023, and the SEC charged founder Abraham Shafi with fraud in Jul 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/31/founder-behind-social-media-app-irl-charged-with-fraud)). The founder disputes the 95% figure.
- **Lesson:** for an IRL product, downloads and MAU are vanity metrics. The real measure is attendance at events, which cannot be faked.

### College / campus
- **Fizz:** about 3.5M MAU on 300+ campuses (early 2025). Reportedly raised $41M in 2025, and a Jul 2026 strategic investment at a $200M cap (BI, via search snippets). Growth is gated on .edu verification and goes campus by campus.
- **Ditto** (AI date-matching for college students): about 150k users at UCSD, Berkeley, UCLA, USC, Michigan and UT. One match every Wednesday. $9.2M raised. Users quintupled after its funding news. Free for now; users say they would pay $15–20 per date (Forbes Italia).
- **Known** (date-setting): **$15 per date, no-show rate under 1%.** $9.7M round led by Forerunner (Forbes Italia).

### Other 2025–26 entrants
- **Clyx:** $14M Series A in Sep 2025 (Blitzscaling). At the time of the raise it had 50k active ticket-buying users and 200k browsing, in only 2 cities (Miami, London). It added NYC, Reykjavik and LA (Feb 2026). It pulls in Ticketmaster and TikTok events, shows which contacts are going, and suggests people to meet ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/this-gen-z-founded-app-just-raised-14m-to-take-on-the-loneliness-episdemic)).
- **Les Amís** (women and LGBTQ+): weekly matches on Mondays and paid membership ($70 in NYC, €55 in Amsterdam). Present in 6 EU cities plus Austin and NYC.
- **Washed Up** (LA, 2026): event discovery plus small-group "plans".
- **Market size:** in 2025, a dozen or more local friendship apps had about **$16M in US consumer spend and 4.3M downloads** (Appfigures via TechCrunch 2026-04-05). That is tiny next to dating apps (Match Q1 2026 revenue $864M). Most of the money is in tickets, food and venues, not app subscriptions.

## 2. Patterns (what works)

1. **Charge at the door (or by subscription). A low price is the no-show filter.** Timeleft (€18M ARR at about €13–20 per ticket or month), 222 (several $M/yr, near cash-flow positive at $22.22), Known (<1% no-shows at $15) and Clockout ($1.1M ARR at $99/yr) all charge. The free friendship apps (Bumble BFF/Geneva at $0 revenue; Pie) make nothing. A small payment works as a commitment device. It is not the profit center, because food, venues and tickets carry most of the spend.
2. **A fixed ritual on a fixed day, in small groups.** Timeleft runs Wednesday 7pm tables of 5–6. 222 runs tables of 6. Ditto sends a match every Wednesday, Les Amís every Monday, and Thursday is built on Thursdays. Strava reports group runs of 10+ up 18%. A fixed weekly slot turns attendance into a habit, and Timeleft's average use rose from 1.4 to 2.2 per month.
3. **Density before breadth.** Timeleft's rule is 50+ weekly participants per city, and it cut from 325 cities to about 220. Pie's traction is concentrated in Chicago, NYC and LA. Clyx raised $14M on just 2 cities. Fizz expanded campus by campus to 300+. The product is the pool of people available in one city on one night.
4. **Bring in independent hosts instead of producing every event.** Posh has 50k organizers and a $350M GMV. Pie pays hosts $5–10 per RSVP. Tinder's events are run by third parties and reached 71% of active 18–24s in LA. Hinge funds recurring groups with up to $25k each. Meetup is the opposite case: it raised prices on hosts, and organizers left.
5. **Showing which friends are going drives engagement.** Pie's time in app went from 2 to 10 minutes after it showed contacts attending. Clyx is built on contacts. Partiful (500k MAU, +400%) grows because every invite brings in new users. Clockout users sent ~400k invites. People are more willing to go if they can bring a friend or see friends going; 222 allows plus-ones for this reason.
6. **An activity gives people a reason to show up.** Run clubs grew 3.5x (2025) after +59% (2024). 66% of Gen Z made friends through fitness, and 1 in 5 dated someone from a run club. Supper clubs have 10k+ waitlists, and Timeleft is built around dinner. "Meeting people" is the hidden benefit, and the stated purpose is food or fitness, which avoids the awkwardness of joining an app to find friends.
7. **Paid ads work for a paid, ritual format with quick payback.** Timeleft expanded only through ads, with no ambassadors, and opened a city only if CAC worked. Posh and Partiful grow organically through organizers and invites. Contrast IRL, which spent $6M on incentivized installs and ended with 95% bots.
8. **Incumbents are moving into IRL, which validates demand but squeezes standalone apps.** Tinder Events reached 26 cities, Hinge has given $2.3M in grants, and Bumble BFF is unmonetized. Eventbrite (revenue down 10%, sold for $500M) and Meetup (sold to Bending Spoons) show generic tools are losing. Winners own a niche (nightlife: Posh; tech: Luma; invites: Partiful).

## 3. The 3 biggest traps

1. **Vanity metrics in place of attendance.** IRL claimed 12M users and 95% were bots ($170M raised, now fraud charges). Meetup's member counts include dead accounts. Pie had 300k users but was called "mostly a directory". The metric that matters is paid seats filled and repeat rate.
2. **Expanding before a city has enough people, and running out of new faces.** Timeleft opened 325 cities and many fizzled; users got bored meeting the same ~10 people. Thursday had 2M users and still killed its app. Small markets quickly run through the available strangers.
3. **A free app for making friends, with no urgency and no payment.** Geneva made $0 revenue and Bumble does not even try to monetize BFF. Friendship feels less urgent than dating, and few people want to admit they need an app for friends (The Hustle). The total US market is only about $16M. The money is in tickets, food, venues and brand sponsorship, so a business has to sit on that transaction (as Posh does) or charge for a ritual (as Timeleft and 222 do).
