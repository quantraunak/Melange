**Pattern.** Recent buyers fall into four groups: (a) **dating incumbents buying niche communities or new formats** (Match: HER, Overtone; Bumble: Geneva); (b) **vertical community platforms buying tools** (Strava: Runna, Breakaway); (c) **app conglomerates and holdcos buying audiences cheaply** (Voodoo: BeReal; 9count: Lex; Ithaca: Bumble); (d) **AI labs acqui-hiring consumer and personalization teams** (OpenAI). ## 2. Synthesis: 7 trends, and what a product needs in 2028 to ride each

**T1. Paid swiping is in structural decline; intent and identity filters still grow.**
Evidence: Bumble payers -16%, Tinder payers about -5%, Match payers -6%, Bumble sold at about 10% of its IPO value. Meanwhile Hinge +22%, Grindr +32%, and Match buying HER.
*2028 product needs:* a narrow, self-selecting community where membership itself signals intent or identity; monetization per payer, not per swipe; a reason to leave the app (meeting up) built into the core loop.

**T2. Friends left the feed; the social graph moved into group chats and DMs.**
Evidence: Instagram friend content went from 11% to 7% of time (Meta's FTC exhibit). Mosseri says DMs exceed Stories, which exceed feed. 79% share life updates with the group chat first (Giphy). Yope raised $12.3M and Series raised $5.1M, both built on private graphs or iMessage.
*2028 product needs:* group-first rather than profile-first design; it should work inside or next to existing chats (iMessage, WhatsApp, shareable links) instead of asking people to rebuild their graph; small-n sharing with no public counts.

**T3. The "reset to real": IRL is the scarce good, and the phone becomes the logistics layer.**
Evidence: Strava run clubs 3.5x and 1M new clubs; Eventbrite phone-free events +567%, US attendance +913%; 79% of 18–35 plan more events in 2026; Posh $22M, Pie $11.5M; 1 in 5 dated someone from a run club.
*2028 product needs:* the success metric is meetups that actually happened, not minutes in the app; recurring formats (clubs, weekly slots) rather than one-off events; host and organizer tools; low time-in-app by design.

**T4. Loneliness peaks at 18–29, so "help me make friends" is a mass-market job.**
Evidence: Gallup about 30% of 18–29 lonely (vs 21% of all adults) and rising since 2024; Cigna Gen Z 67%; young US men 25% daily loneliness.
*2028 product needs:* friendship framed as a side effect of doing something (an activity, a project, a shared interest) rather than a "friend-finding" app, which carries stigma; repeated exposure to the same small group; an obvious answer to "who else is going."

**T5. AI floods the content layer, so verified-human and provenance become features.**
Evidence: 75% of Gen Z want AI content labeled; 54% want no AI in creative work; Pew finds 50% think AI worsens relationships vs 5% better; AI-video feeds spike and decay (Sora -45% in a month).
*2028 product needs:* proof that a real person met another real person, or made the thing (proof-of-meetup, real-name or attested credits, camera-original capture); AI used behind the scenes, never as fake people in the feed.

**T6. AI should introduce humans, not replace them; AI companions are becoming a liability.**
Evidence: 72% of teens have tried companions, and spending runs above $300M a year, but Character.AI banned under-18s after deaths, and Pew is negative on AI and relationships. a16z (Kim, Elman) wants "AI that makes tech more social / gets people together IRL." Match spun out Overtone (AI and voice dating).
*2028 product needs:* AI as matchmaker, planner or concierge (who to meet, when, where, an icebreaker) whose output is a human interaction; age gating and safety from day one; no persona bots posing as users.

**T7. Consumer capital is scarce, and buyers pay for engaged niche audiences plus tools, not for scale.**
Evidence: consumer-social VC was $3.1B in 2021 and $0.9B in 2024; consumer is about 4% of YC's 2026 batch. Exits are mid-size: BeReal €500M, Strava's tuck-ins, Match's niche buys, Bumble $1.3B at a trough.
*2028 product needs:* revenue early (supply side or host side pays); retention numbers that hold without paid acquisition; a dense niche an incumbent would want (vertical community plus workflow tools); a clean data and safety record.

## 3. Acquirers actively buying consumer social (2024–2026)

1. **Match Group:** HER (2025), Overtone spinout (2025–26); historically The League (about $30M), Hyperconnect ($1.7B). Buys niche communities and new formats.
2. **Bumble → now Ithaca Holdings:** Bumble bought Geneva (2024). Ithaca (Gupta/Hegeman, Sequoia-linked, raising $1B or more) bought Bumble for $1.3B (Oct 2026) and is a new holdco that will likely add tuck-ins.
3. **Strava:** Runna, The Breakaway (2025); IPO pitched as funding for "more and bigger acquisitions." Buys tools that serve a community.
4. **Voodoo:** BeReal, €500M (2024). Gaming studio buying Gen Z audiences.
5. **9count (Alex Hofmann):** Lex (2024). Consumer-app roll-up.
6. **OpenAI:** Roi, Sky, Statsig (2025); runs Sora as a social app. Acqui-hires consumer teams.
7. **Grindr:** cash-generative (42% margin) and pushing AI features; a plausible buyer of queer and community adjacencies (no confirmed 2025–26 deal found).
8. Long tail: commerce or delivery apps buying social-discovery apps (Snoonu–BiteSight); AI-data firms acqui-hiring social-app teams (Mecka–Thirdspace).

## 4. Gaps and caveats
- No primary 2025–26 consumer-social thesis was found for Sequoia, Lightspeed, General Catalyst or NFX. YC RFS findings come from secondary sources only.
- Several attitude numbers come from surveys commissioned by interested companies (BeReal, Giphy, Eventbrite, Strava). Treat these as directional.
- Film camera and dumb-phone data is weak; there are no reliable 2025 sales numbers.
- The Tinder-specific payer count (8.5M) is unconfirmed against the press-release table; the company-wide 13.25M figure is.
