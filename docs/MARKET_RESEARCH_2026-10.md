# Is Melange a business? Evidence, October 2026

Research run 2026-10-19 across three independent threads: what creatives
complain about, what tools already exist, and what has been tried and died.
Every claim below has a source. Where something could not be verified, it says
so.

**Conclusion: no, not in its current shape.** Not because of execution. The
market has a structure that forecloses it.

## The structural problem

The core transaction — a photographer and a model shooting together for free,
for portfolio — **has no money in it**. There is no payment, so there is no
take rate to defend and nothing to capture. Instagram DMs are not a leak from
Melange; they are the correct medium for arranging a free favour between two
people. Six users producing zero collaborations is the market's known answer,
not a failure of the product.

## The decisive finding: PurplePort

purpleport.com has run this exact market since September 2010. Homepage
counters, read from raw HTML on 2026-10-19:

| Casting calls created | 385,643 |
| Messages sent | 29,581,588 |
| References exchanged | 902,921 |
| New members last month | 2,923 |

It is free. It includes a shoot planner, casting calls, search, and a
reputation system with a published late-cancellation and no-show policy.

**What it charges for is the whole lesson.** Free accounts cannot create or
apply to *paid* casting calls. The paywall sits exactly at the transition to
money. Sixteen years of revealed preference from the people who know this
market best: unpaid collaboration is a loss leader, and only proximity to a
budget is worth charging for. That is precisely the half Melange does.

Implied VIP price ~$5/month (not directly verified, behind login; inferred
from a referral reward redeemable as "30 days VIP" OR "$5 USD"). Two of its
four VIP help articles are guides to getting VIP **without paying**.

## The ceiling, if we won completely

BLS 2025 (bls.gov/ooh): photographers median pay **$44,660/yr**, **145,000
jobs**, **67% self-employed**, outlook **−1%**.

145,000 × $120/yr (a $10/mo product) = **$17.4M ARR at 100% penetration of
every photographer in America.** The non-client editorial/TFP slice is a
minority of that population and its poorest segment.

One working wedding photographer on his whole stack: *"I use iCal, mail, we
transfer and wave app — annual subscription for all is $12, I love it."*

## Two legal constraints, both in our jurisdiction

1. **Charging the supply side for opportunity access is being litigated in
   California.** Two class actions (LA Superior Court, 2024) against Casting
   Networks and Actors Access under Labor Code §450. A petition against
   Casting Networks' pricing has 7,223 signatures. This closes the obvious
   monetization for an introductions product.
2. **Section 230 does not shield a negligent failure-to-warn claim here.**
   *Jane Doe No. 14 v. Internet Brands* (9th Cir., 767 F.3d 894): men used
   Model Mayhem to lure aspiring models to fake auditions and assaulted them.
   The court held §230 did not bar the failure-to-warn claim. Melange
   introduces young women to strangers who invite them to private shoots. That
   exposure is real and uninsured.

## The graveyard

- **Meero** raised ~$293M, shut the photographer marketplace, found no buyer
  for the remainder.
- **Model Mayhem** lost roughly **50x of its liquidity** to Instagram. Its own
  forums: *"I used to see 10-20 casting calls PER WEEK... Now I'd be shocked
  if there were that many PER YEAR."*
- **Cult Creative** had 13,000 creators and 1,100 brands and killed the app
  anyway. The founder's reasons: the tech could not track outcomes after the
  match or handle getting people paid. It pivoted **toward workflow and money,
  away from matching**.
- **Collective Voice** (140k creators, 22k brands, 14 years) wound down 2026.
- ~90% of transactions in online freelance marketplaces move off-platform once
  two parties are matched (Gu & Zhu, *Management Science* 67(2), 2021).

Supply has also partly exited the category: models report making several times
more from direct content than from shoots.

## What survived, and why

Every survivor broke the peer-to-peer matching frame:

- **soona** owns crew and studios, sells finished assets ($39/photo,
  $93/video). Its acquired subsidiary Trend has 3,700 active creators and
  **15,000 on a waitlist** — proof that creative supply is free and abundant
  and **paying demand is the only scarce asset**.
- **Peerspace** made supply a physical thing with a calendar, which cannot be
  DM'd around. 20% host fee.
- **Backstage / Talent Systems** charge the aspirational side for access and
  take **zero** percent of bookings, so disintermediation is irrelevant.
- **Pixieset** gives away a CRM and bundles everything at $28/mo to 1M+ users.
- **Flodesk** is the existence proof for a bootstrapped technical founder:
  profitable 14 days after launch, $1M ARR in 4 months, no funding, no sales
  team. Flat $35/mo, with distribution embedded in the product's own output.

## The one place with money attached

A fashion photographer **quit the career** over exactly Melange's stated
problem (r/photography 2025-05-25): *"Is it silly to have quit photography
simply because of how much of a pain and hassle it was to organize shoots and
find models?"* The replies agree that 80-90% of the work is logistics.

But the solution that community pays for is **not software**. It is
**organized shoots and workshops**: someone books the model, MUA, styling and
location, and photographers pay to show up and shoot. Local, needs one city
and an Instagram account, monetizes the precise pain.

Real, but a services business, not a venture one.

## Cheapest disconfirming experiments, if revisiting

1. Price three local photography workshops and count fill rates. That is the
   demand curve and it was not reached.
2. Log in to PurplePort and Kavyar (Cloudflare-blocked to research) and read
   what the non-client segment already does for free.

## Not verified

Tracxn) are directional only.
